Teaching a laptop to play Breakout from pixels with PPO

An agent sees only the screen: four stacked 84×84 grayscale frames. It has to learn to move the paddle and break bricks. It is trained with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) on an 8-core laptop CPU with no GPU, for 40 million game frames (about 185 hours of play at 60 frames per second; first planned as 10 million, see the log). The goal: a mean score of at least 300 over 10 full games (all 5 lives, unclipped score) on seeds it never trained on. Every run is tracked in MLflow; this page is rebuilt from that data every 15 minutes.

🎯 Result: goal met

The final agent (40 M frames) averaged 391.6 over the 10 fixed goal-check games (full games, all 5 lives, real score; every game between 341 and 424). On 100 fresh games it averaged 407.6 ± 6.7, with 99 of 100 games at 300 or more. The rules for this test were written down before the run ended (see the log). Training took 9.6 hours on the laptop CPU. The model is on the v1.0 release.

Goal table

Every checkpoint plays 10 full games (5 lives, real score) on seeds it never trained on, under two protocols: full game (nothing happens at a lost life; strict) and training wrappers (a lost life is an episode boundary with a soft reset and automatic FIRE, as in training). The 95% interval is a t-interval for the mean of the 10 games.

Training diagnostics from the MLflow run, exported to JSON

PPO's health signals per update: the approximate KL divergence between the old and new policy, the fraction of samples whose probability ratio was clipped, the policy's entropy (how random it still is), the value loss, and how well the critic explains the returns. Throughput is in agent steps per second (1 step = 4 frames).
