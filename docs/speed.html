Speed

Everything runs on one laptop: a laptop Intel i5-8250U (4 physical cores, 8 threads), 7 GB of RAM, no GPU. The plan said: measure first, and only port code to a faster language if the training budget would not fit in about 12 hours. 10 M frames fit in under 3 hours; when the budget was raised to 40 M frames (see the log) that became about 9 hours, still inside the limit, so no port was needed. Every number below was measured on this machine.

1. The simulator: envpool vs gymnasium

Random actions, Breakout with the standard wrappers, 8 seconds per setting. envpool runs the emulators in C++ threads and does the preprocessing there; gymnasium's AsyncVectorEnv uses one Python process per game.

1 agent step = 4 game frames. At 4,000 steps per second the simulator alone could play 10 M frames in about 10 minutes, so the simulator is not what limits training.

2. The whole PPO loop: where the time goes

One PPO iteration = rollout (8 games × 128 steps, the network picks every action) + update (4 epochs × 4 minibatches of 256 samples, forward and backward through the CNN). Measured with PyTorch on CPU at different thread counts:

The update takes about three quarters of the time: the backward pass through the convolutions is the real cost. More PyTorch threads don't help beyond 4: the CPU has only 4 physical cores (8 with hyperthreading), envpool's 8 threads already want the cores during the rollout and the small batches don't split well. Chosen: 4 PyTorch threads, which leaves cores free for this site and the videos.
